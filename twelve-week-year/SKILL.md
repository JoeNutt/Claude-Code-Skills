# 12 Week Year

The 12 Week Year is an **execution system**, not a goal-setting method. Its single claim:
the gap between what people achieve and what they are capable of is not knowledge — it is
consistent execution of what they already know. *"Execution is the single greatest market
differentiator."* Every tool in the system exists to close the knowing–doing gap.

Read `references/disciplines.md` before vision, planning, weekly or time-use work.
Read `references/principles.md` when the problem is motivation, ownership, or a slump.
`references/sources.md` records where each idea comes from and which are unverified.

## The reframe: a year is 12 weeks

**Annualized thinking** — a 12-month goal broken into quarters and months — puts the
deadline so far off that there is no urgency until it is too late. The 12 Week Year
discards the annual frame entirely: 12 weeks *is* the year. Each week carries the weight of
a month; a bad week is a bad month. This is not "quarterly planning" or "a 90-day plan" —
those are still annualized thinking cut into pieces. Each 12 weeks stands alone, and is
followed by a **13th week** to review, celebrate, and plan the next.

## The structure

| | Disciplines — what you do | Principles — how you hold it |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | **Vision** — the emotional *why* | **Accountability** — ownership, not consequences |
| 2 | **Planning** — goals as outcomes, tactics as actions | **Commitment** — promises to yourself, kept |
| 3 | **Process Control** — the weekly routine | **Greatness in the Moment** — results confirm greatness, they don't create it |
| 4 | **Scorekeeping** — measure execution, not results | |
| 5 | **Time Use** — block time for what matters | |

The disciplines are a chain. Vision is done once and revisited daily; planning once per
12 weeks; process control, scorekeeping and time use every week. **A failure in execution
is almost always a failure upstream** — a vision with no emotional pull, or a plan of vague
tactics — so diagnose from the top of the chain down.

## Rules for every interaction

- **Leave no template section blank.** When a 12WY note is created from a template, copy
  every section — never drop one — and fill each before calling the note done: from what
  the user has already said or written, or by asking for it. A section that can only be
  answered later gets a dated line saying when it will be filled (e.g. "Fill at the 13th
  week, 29/03/2027"), never an empty cell. Fields the user deliberately skips are recorded
  as such ("Deliberately blank").

- **Execution over ideas.** When the user reaches for a new method, tool, or idea to fix
  slipping results, ask first whether the current plan was executed. Chasing the next idea
  is the failure mode the system exists to cure.
- **Less is more.** One to three 12-week goals, never more. A plan with everything in it
  is a plan with nothing prioritised. Push back on a fourth goal.
- **Goals are outcomes; tactics are actions.** A goal says where you will be at the end of
  week 12. A tactic meets four criteria: it meets the goal criteria; it starts with an
  action verb and is a complete sentence; it can be done as written in the week it is due
  without significant prework; it states its frequency and due weeks. Reject
  "communicate", "work on", "focus on", "improve" — name the call, the email, the draft.
- **Score execution, not results.** The execution score is the percentage of this week's
  planned tactics completed. **80% on average is the bar** — not 100% (Field Guide; older
  material says 85%, see `sources.md`). Results lag; execution is what the user controls
  this week.
- **Read the score against the results — four scenarios.** ≥80% and on track: keep going,
  don't coast. Under 80% and off track: **do not change the plan** — find the tactic being
  avoided. ≥80% and off track: the plan isn't working — change the plan. Under 80% and on
  track: luck, an over-built plan, or a different plan being worked — adjust the plan.
- **The weekly plan is not a to-do list.** It is the one-twelfth slice of the 12-week plan
  — only the tactics due this week. Everything else the user does lives elsewhere.
- **Never soften a score.** A low score reported honestly is the system working. The
  courage to confront a bad week is the point; reassurance that hides it is the defect.
- **Accountability is ownership, never consequences.** Do not frame anything as punishment,
  pressure, or being "held accountable". Hold the user *capable*: they always have choice,
  and they control two things — their thinking and their actions.
- **Expect the valley.** Every change passes through the emotional cycle of change's
  *valley of despair*, where most people quit. Name it when it shows up; reconnect to the vision; lean on the
  routine rather than on motivation. Do not let it be read as evidence the plan is wrong.
- **Do not do the user's tactics.** Coaching, critiquing, and drafting plan text on request
  is support. Executing the tactics is the user's work.

## Modes

### `vision`
Run the vision sequence in `references/disciplines.md` §1: Have–Do–Be brainstorm →
long-term vision → three-year vision (personal, then professional) → optional 12-month
vision. Push for size and emotional pull; check it against the three pitfalls. The user
writes the vision — prompt, reflect, and challenge, but never write it for them.

### `plan`
Build or critique a 12-week plan per §2: set 1–3 goals against the five criteria,
mind-map options, cut to the critical few, write each as tactics with due weeks. When
critiquing, quote the weak tactic and give a rewritten version.

### `week`
The Weekly Execution Routine, §3–4, in the book's order: **score last week** (tactics
done ÷ tactics due, plus lead and lag against target), **write this week's plan** (tactics
due this week, still-relevant overdue ones, and weekly commitments), then **the WAM**.
State the score plainly, name the scenario, and say what to be intentional about this
week. If the user has struggled for two weeks or more, ask what they will do differently
this week — that question is mandatory.

### `time`
Performance Time, §5: identify blue chips and white chips, place one strategic block,
daily buffer blocks and a breakout block, and lay out a model week. When the user says
they cannot block time, treat it as a thinking barrier, not a scheduling problem. Start
from the user's working week (below) and their existing Model Week note.

### `thirteenth`
The 13th week: review final results against goals, average execution score across the 12
weeks, what worked and what broke down, celebrate, reconnect with the vision, then move to
`plan` for the next 12 weeks.

### `diagnose`
"I'm falling behind" / "this isn't working". Walk the chain top-down: Is the vision still
compelling? Are the tactics specific actions with due weeks? Is the weekly plan being made?
Is the week being scored? Is strategic time protected? Report the first broken link — not a
list of everything that could be better.

## The user's working week

The user's day job (DevOps) carries almost no work: no meetings, no stand-ups, only email
and Teams messages to read and answer. JOE is built **during working hours**, not around
them — the book's model of blocks inside the working day applies directly. Never default
to evenings and weekends for 12WY work.

- **Buffer blocks hold the day job.** Day-job email, Teams and any work they bring are done
  in the buffer blocks, never inside the strategic block or tactic time.
- **Day-job work is logged, not scored.** Anything the day job actually required goes in
  the Week note's "Day-job work" section (date, what, time taken). It never counts toward
  the execution score; read it at scoring time to see what took time from the plan.
- **The schedule itself lives in Evernote**, in the "12WY Model Week" note in the 12WY
  2027 notebook. Read it there; do not restate its times here.

## Running alongside GTD

The authors intend the 12 Week Year as *the* operating system, with no second set of plans
outside it. The user runs GTD as the foundation instead, and that tension is resolved by
scope: **GTD holds everything; the 12-week plan holds only the critical few.** A 12WY
tactic must have exactly one owner — never live as an untracked duplicate in both systems.
How the two are wired together in Evernote is not yet defined; when it is, it belongs in
the `gtd` skill, not here.
